# Educational quality and pronunciation

Lessons use accessible Egyptian Arabic explanations and original English examples
in shopping, transport, appointments and workplace contexts. Each word contains a
selected meaning, context, POS link, three different translated sentences, a usage
pattern and error note, mental association, recall, recognition, completion,
construction and pronunciation self-comparison. Mental associations need no image;
no stock imagery or fabricated image licences are included.

Examples progress from everyday use to a different context/structure and a longer
practical sentence. Longer is not automatically better: a linguistic reviewer
must check natural British usage, accurate Arabic meaning and the taught sense.
Structural validation checks presence and distinctness, not educational truth.
Open construction requires learner or human judgement; no claim of automatic
semantic grading is made. Recognition distractors come from other lesson meanings;
a pilot should test whether these are sufficiently discriminating.

The stress, silent-letter notes and spelling guides have now been checked against
Cambridge UK written pronunciation references. They carry `text_reference_checked`,
not final `verified`; see [the review evidence](VERIFICATION.md). IPA and Arabic
pseudo-transcription remain null. Spelling groups are accessible training aids,
not phoneme boundaries or universally fixed syllable counts. Independent/audio
review is still pending; do not claim reference-quality audio or tested learning
efficacy from a written-reference check alone.

Use Modern Standard Southern British English as an initial teaching reference;
recognise legitimate variation in British speech. Candidate listening assessments
include P/B, V/F, short/long vowel distinctions, TH sounds, schwa, stress, linking
and intonation. They are candidates, never assumed diagnoses of a learner.
Language CEFR and pronunciation starting point remain independent.

When licensed references are available, implement and review the sequence:
listen → discriminate → understand articulation → imitate → optionally record →
compare → speak a full sentence. Each stage needs actual material; listing this
sequence in JSON does not provide a listening contrast or reference recording.
Those activities are explicitly blocked/deferred in this snapshot. Add individually
verified contrast recordings and practical mouth-position guidance for observed
needs. Do not require IPA; introduce a symbol only with a familiar audible example.

Normal, slow and repeat playback are future capability metadata, not working
controls. Slow playback should preserve pitch where supported and must be tested
on iPhone Safari. Human recordings need origin, creator, accent, file format,
licence evidence and attribution before becoming available. Synthetic alternatives
must be labelled synthetic; browser speech synthesis is not the primary reference.
No asset may be called an Oxford human recording without evidence. Sentence audio
is independently licensed and currently null for all examples.

Record only after explicit microphone permission, locally, with deletion controls.
Self-comparison does not establish objective accuracy; do not turn it into an accent
score. Audio absence or permission denial must not erase comprehension/usage work.
Practise intelligible speech without promising accent elimination.

Before release, a qualified British-English pronunciation reviewer and an Egyptian
Arabic reviewer should check all twenty lessons, document corrections and approve
only the reviewed content version. Learner testing then determines where concise
explanations need more support. An assistant bilingual/editorial review has happened; independent human review and learner testing have not.
