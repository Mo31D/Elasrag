# Reference and editorial review — 12 November 2026

All twenty Cambridge UK pronunciation pages were successfully read through the
workspace HTTP client. `data/verification.json` records each exact URL, private
page hash, reviewed lesson hash and a concise finding. Written IPA/sound breakdowns
were used to check stress and the explanations; dictionary pages, definitions,
example sentences and audio are not republished. IPA remains optional and null.
A source-page hash identifies the observed response, not a permanent upstream
version; the URL may change. The ignored research pages are not a public artifact.

This is an assistant reference/editorial review, not a qualified human signoff,
recording audition or learner trial. `text_reference_checked` is intentionally
separate from final `verified`. Cambridge's UK label supports the segmental/stress
checks, but does not certify every recording as Modern Standard Southern British.

## Corrections and retained distinctions

- **convenient:** four careful syllables, with second-syllable stress; corrected the
  old three-group guide. Grouping is an accessible spelling guide, not IPA slicing.
- **acknowledge:** replaced a misleading claim about silent k with one consonant
  sound for the written ck group before n. The first example now explicitly
  acknowledges receipt of the email; receipt confirmation is not approval.
- **additional:** ti is the relevant spelling group here, rather than a simplistic
  tion rule. Weak vowels may compress in natural speech.
- **suitable:** acknowledge both UK initial variants; do not mark one wrong.
- **require:** retain variable final realisation rather than assert a rigid syllable
  count; explain context-dependent final r. The spelling guide has two groups,
  which is not a claim that every careful pronunciation has two vowel nuclei.
- **receipt / debt:** silent p / b checked against the UK sound sequences.
- **appropriate:** verify the adjective; its ending differs from the verb.
- **confirm / concern / deliver:** non-rhotic r explanations remain explicitly
  tied to context and the selected model, not all British accents.
- **reliable / accurate / budget / queue:** made weak-vowel, transition and
  letter-group explanations more precise and accessible.

All 60 English/Arabic example pairs, 20 sense explanations, usage patterns and
exercise keys were read for meaning, grammar and practical British context. Arabic
for **concern** now includes an important matter as well as anxiety, matching the
safety example. **capable** uses a corrected feminine Arabic adjective. Recognition
keys/options were updated with the changed meaning. No external Arabic translation
was copied, and no claim of native-speaker review is made. The remaining original
examples were retained after review; a longer sentence alone is not evidence of
advanced usefulness.

Review hashes prevent later lesson edits from silently retaining the same audit.
They do not prove linguistic truth. Update the review only after checking the
changed content; do not use a script to rubber-stamp unreviewed modifications.

Before accepting audio: listen to the complete file, confirm word/POS, natural
human origin, British accent suitability and sound quality, validate file/hash and
rights, retain attribution, and test available formats on iPhone Safari in the later
audio phase. No synthetic or mismatched-accent recording is substituted silently.
